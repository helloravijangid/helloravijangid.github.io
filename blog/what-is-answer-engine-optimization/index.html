What Is Answer Engine Optimization?

9 min read · by Ravi Jangid

Answer engine optimization, usually shortened to AEO, is the practice of becoming the source an AI model cites when it answers a question. Not the link someone clicks. The source it quotes, names, or recommends inside the answer itself.

That distinction sounds academic until you look at what it does to a funnel. A buyer who asks a model for a shortlist and gets three names has already narrowed the market before visiting a single site. If you are not one of the three, no amount of ranking on page one recovers that.

AEO and SEO are not competing disciplines

The most common misreading is that AEO replaces SEO. It does not, and the reason is mechanical. AI engines are not discovering the web independently. They lean heavily on what classic search has already crawled, indexed and judged trustworthy. If a page cannot be crawled, it will not be cited either.

So the foundations stay the same. Crawlability, site structure, internal linking, page speed, authority. What changes is the emphasis on top of those foundations.

What models need that rankings never demanded

Four things separate a page that ranks from a page that gets cited.

  • Unambiguous entities. A model has to know what you are, who you serve, and how you relate to the other names in your category. Vague positioning that a human can infer from context is a dead end for a machine.
  • Extractable claims. Answers are assembled from statements that can be lifted cleanly. A specific, self contained sentence gets quoted. A paragraph that only makes sense after the three before it does not.
  • Structured data. Schema is how you state facts in a form that needs no interpretation. It is the cheapest AEO work available and the most commonly skipped.
  • Third party corroboration. Models weight agreement. Being described the same way on review platforms, comparison pages and listicles matters more than saying it louder on your own site.

Why volume is the wrong roadmap input

Traditional keyword research sorts by search volume. For AEO that is close to useless, because the questions that feed an AI recommendation are often long, conversational, and low volume.

A better input is the citation gap: questions your buyers ask where a competitor is cited and you are not. You find them by running the questions, not by pulling a volume report. It is slower, and it produces a much shorter roadmap.

What good looks like

On one B2B fintech engagement, this programme took AI citations from a zero baseline past 62,000, at an average position of roughly five inside AI answers. Organic keywords and referring domains compounded over the same period, which is the point: the two feed each other.

The honest caveat is that AEO is not fast in the way a technical fix is fast. Entity and schema corrections can surface in answers quicker than rankings move, because you are correcting how a model understands you rather than out competing a page. But corroboration takes months, and anyone quoting a fixed date is guessing.

Where to start

Start with the baseline, not the fixes. Until you have scored a fixed set of buyer questions across the major engines, every change you make is unmeasured. Once the scoreboard exists, the roadmap tends to write itself.
