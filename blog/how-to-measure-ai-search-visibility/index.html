How to Measure AI Search Visibility

7 min read · by Ravi Jangid

Most teams asking about AI search have no baseline. They have a feeling that they are not showing up, and a screenshot of one prompt where a competitor got named. That is not a measurement, and you cannot build a roadmap on it.

Here is the method I use before touching anything on a client site.

Build a fixed prompt set

Between forty and eighty questions, written the way a buyer would actually ask them. Not keywords. Questions.

  • Category questions: what a buyer asks when they do not yet know the vendors.
  • Comparison questions: your brand against a named competitor.
  • Problem questions: the symptom they feel before they know the category exists.
  • Qualifier questions: pricing, integrations, compliance, region.

The set has to be fixed. If you change the questions between runs, you have destroyed your ability to compare, and you will spend the next quarter arguing about whether anything improved.

Score every run the same way

Run the full set across each engine you care about. For most B2B work that means Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini. Score each result on three levels, because they are worth very different amounts.

  • Mentioned. Your name appears somewhere in the answer.
  • Cited. Your page is linked or named as a source.
  • Recommended. You are put forward as an answer to the question, not just referenced in passing.

Record which competitors appear alongside you every time. The competitor set an engine returns is frequently not the one the sales team believes it is competing against, and that alone often justifies the exercise.

Turn the scores into a gap map

Sort the questions by where a competitor is recommended and you are absent. That is your citation gap, and it becomes the roadmap, ordered by commercial value rather than search volume.

This is the step most AEO advice skips. Without it you are optimising in the dark and reporting on vibes.

Re run on a fixed cadence

Monthly is enough for most sites. Answers drift, models update, and a single run tells you nothing about direction. What you are watching is the trend line on citations and the shrinking of the gap list.

Doing this by hand is miserable, which is why I wire it to agents that pull from Search Console, GA4, SE Ranking, Bing Webmaster and DataForSEO and run the scoring continuously. The measurement is worthless if it is too much work to repeat.

Tie it to pipeline or do not bother

Citations are a means, not the goal. Segment AI referral sessions in analytics, watch what they do on the site, and connect them to signups. On one engagement that scoreboard tracked citations past 62,000 while showing which engines actually sent people who converted. Those were not the engines anyone expected.
